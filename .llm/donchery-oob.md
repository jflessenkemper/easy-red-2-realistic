### SHAKY / INFERRED

- **What Kradschützen-Bataillon 2 was doing at 16:00 on 13 May.** I found **no source at any level** that places K.2 at the Donchery crossing site. See §1b — this is the one you need to act on.
- **Number of rifle companies in K.2.** Bayonetstrength says the 1st/2nd/3rd Pz.Div. Kradschützen battalions nominally had **two** motorcycle rifle companies, but that "the *Kradschützen* Battalion of 2nd Panzer Division however appears to have been authorised a **third** Rifle Company." Lexikon-der-Wehrmacht lists **six** company field-post numbers for K.2, which would fit 3 rifle + MG + heavy + column — but field-post lists span the whole war, so this is not proof for May 1940. Treat 3 rifle companies as *probable, not certain*.
- **Kradschützen company total strength (~150).** Derived by me from the platoon tables, not stated in any source.
- **French squad size**: sources say 11 *or* 12 men per *groupe de combat*. Both are in circulation; 12 (incl. sergeant + corporal) is the commoner figure.
- **47mm SA 37 APX at Donchery**: I could **not** confirm any were present. Do not place them.
- **Exact garrison strength of III/147e RIF on 13 May**: not found. A fortress MG battalion is not a standard rifle battalion and was smaller in riflemen.

### CONFLICTS FOUND

1. **"16 Panzer 38(t)" vs "16 Panzerbefehlswagen".** One aggregator page renders the 2. Pz.Div. row with 16 in a `38(t)` column; panzerworld's table renders the same 16 as **command tanks (Panzerbefehlswagen)**. The latter is correct — 38(t) equipped 7. and 8. Panzer-Divisions, not the 2nd. **Do not put 38(t) or 35(t) in this mission.**
2. **Crousse's rank**: given as *chef de bataillon* in one source and *capitaine* in another. Minor.
3. **Who actually cleared Donchery's bunkers.** French and German accounts converge on this being done largely by **1. Panzer-Division's Schützen-Regiment 1** (Balck), which crossed at Floing and took the bunker line from the rear / cut the Donchery–Sedan road, rather than by 2. Pz.Div. frontally. Sources differ on emphasis but not on the substance.
4. **Zündapp KS 750 is anachronistic** — see §1d. It was accepted by the Wehrmacht in April 1940 but did not enter series production until **spring 1941**. It was not at Sedan.

## 1. GERMAN SIDE

### 1a. Which division crossed at Donchery

**2. Panzer-Division** (Generalleutnant Rudolf Veiel), part of Guderian's XIX. Armeekorps (mot.).

The three crossing sites on 13 May were divided as follows:

| Site | Division | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Donchery** (downstream / west) | **2. Panzer-Division** | right flank |
| **Gaulier / Floing** (centre) | **1. Panzer-Division** + **Infanterie-Regiment Großdeutschland** + Sturm-Pionier-Bataillon 43 | centre |
| **Wadelincourt / Bazeilles** (upstream / east) | **10. Panzer-Division** (Inf.Rgt. 86) | left flank |

Großdeutschland was attached to **1. Panzer-Division in the centre**, *not* at Donchery. If your mission has GD units, that is wrong for this location.

**What actually happened at Donchery on 13 May:**

- 2. Pz.Div. arrived **late**, strung out in a ~250 km traffic jam through the Ardennes.
- It had **given up its heavy howitzers to 1. Pz.Div.**; only 24 guns arrived at **17:00**, with minimal ammunition. So at 16:00 the Germans at Donchery had **almost no artillery support**.
- Germans held the **north (right) bank** of Donchery from about **13:00**.
- Luftwaffe/Stuka bombardment ran through the morning until about **14:00**, stunning the bunker garrisons.
- The assault crossing then **failed**. French artillery from the far bank destroyed most of the assault boats; German tanks were knocked out. Accounts say only one officer and one man got across, and they swam straight back.
- German tanks were used as **direct-fire bunker-busters from the north bank**, hull-down behind the **railway embankment**, engaging each blockhouse with up to four tanks at once. Despite this, III/147e RIF's fire held the Germans off through the afternoon.
- 1. Pz.Div. (Schützen-Regiment 1 under Balck) crossed at Floing in assault boats in the afternoon, crossed the **Donchery–Sedan railway line around 17:30**, and took the high ground, removing the flanking fire.
- Only then, at **20:00**, did 2. Pz.Div. elements infiltrate the French positions opposite Donchery. Regular ferrying began **22:20**, in darkness. Bridging did not start until **09:00 on 14 May** and took 20 hours under fire.

**Design implication:** at 16:00 the Donchery action is a *failing* opposed river crossing under bunker fire, with German tanks in an overwatch/direct-fire role from the far bank — not a successful blitzkrieg breakthrough.

### 1b. Was Kradschützen-Bataillon 2 at Donchery? — PLAINLY: NOT AS THE LEAD UNIT

**Kradschützen-Bataillon 2 was genuinely 2. Panzer-Division's motorcycle battalion** in May 1940 — that part of your scenario is fine at divisional level. Confirmed in the 2. Pz.Div. 1940 Gliederung (Pz.Rgt. 3, Pz.Rgt. 4, Schützen-Regiment 2, **Kradschützen-Bataillon 2**, Art.Rgt. 74, Aufkl.Abt. 5, Pz.Jg.Abt. 38, Pi.Btl. 38, Nachr.Abt. 38) and in the bayonetstrength Annex B table pairing 2nd Pz.Div. with Schützen-Regiment 2 and Kradschützen-Bataillon 2.

**But no source I could find places K.2 at the Donchery crossing on 13 May.** Its own unit history entry says only "participated in the 1940 western campaign" with no locations. The unit that made the assault crossing at Donchery was **2. Panzer-Division's rifle regiment — Schützen-Regiment 2** — supported by divisional pioneers (Pionier-Bataillon 38) and by the tanks firing from the embankment.

Doctrinally this is exactly what you'd expect. Kradschützen battalions were **advance guard, reconnaissance, flank screen and rapid-exploitation** troops — "rapidly outflank the enemy, seize forward areas and act as fast reconnaissance units". They dismounted to fight, but they were not the formation you tasked with an opposed assault crossing against concrete. That is a Schützen + Pionier job.

**Recommendation — relabel the player side.** Pick one:

1. **Most accurate**: `Schützen-Regiment 2, 2. Panzer-Division` (or `II./Schützen-Regiment 2`). This is the unit that actually attacked across the Meuse at Donchery on 13 May 1940.
2. **If you want to keep motorcycle troops** for the aesthetic: relabel to `Kradschützen-Bataillon 2 (2. Panzer-Division)` but **reframe the mission brief** — e.g. the motorcycle battalion committed to widen the bridgehead / screen the western flank toward Villers-sur-Bar and Pont-à-Bar. That is plausible for a Kradschützen unit even though I cannot document it. Label the scenario blurb as "plausible reconstruction", not "historical".
3. **Do not** keep "Kradschützen-Bataillon 2" as the lead assault-crossing unit. That's the combination that is affirmatively wrong.

### 1c. Kradschützen-Bataillon establishment, 1940

Best available reconstruction is Gary Kennedy's *Notes on the organization of the German Schützen and Kradschützen Battalion 1938 to 1941* (bayonetstrength.uk), built from surviving KStN tables. Note several key KStN tables are **lost**, so parts are reconstructions — Kennedy flags which.

**Battalion structure (1st / 2nd / 3rd Panzer-Divisions pattern):**

- Battalion Headquarters (KStN 1109, "Battalion Headquarters (Motorcycle)") + Communication Detachment
- **2 Motorcycle Rifle Companies** (KStN **1111**, 1 Oct 1937) — *2. Pz.Div. apparently authorised a 3rd*
- **1 Motorcycle MG Company** (KStN 1118) — 2 HMG platoons of 4 MG34 each + a mortar platoon of six 8-cm mortars
- **1 Heavy Company** — Anti-tank Platoon (3× 3.7-cm Pak 35/36, KStN 1122), Infantry Gun Platoon (2× 7.5-cm leIG 18, KStN 1123), Pioneer Platoon (3 squads, 1 LMG each, KStN 1124)

**Battalion totals** (Kennedy's column F = "Kradschützen Battalion of 1st, 2nd and 3rd Panzer Divisions, with three Rifle Companies", 1939 Mobilisation Plan):

- **959 all ranks** (26 officers, 3 officials, 6+132 NCOs, 792 privates)
- Weapons: **31 LMG (MG34), 14 HMG (MG34 on tripod), 9× 5-cm mortars, 6× 8-cm mortars, 2× 7.5-cm leIG 18, 3× 3.7-cm Pak 35/36**
- Small arms: **693 rifles, 266 pistols, and only 2 machine pistols in the entire battalion**

> **Important for weapon loadouts:** MP38s were essentially absent at squad level in May 1940. Two machine pistols per *battalion*. Squad leaders still carried the Kar98k; the MP40 only reached squad leaders around 1941. Do not arm your German squads with MP40s.

**Squad (Gruppe) — KStN 1111, 1 October 1937 — 9 men:**

| Role | Men | Weapons |
|---|---|---|
| Squad leader (NCO) | 1 | rifle |
| Rifleman | 1 | rifle |
| Light machine gunner | 1 | pistol + **MG34** |
| Assistant gunners | 3 | 1 pistol, 2 rifles |
| Motorcycle drivers | 3 | 3 rifles |
| **Total** | **9** | **1× MG34, 2 pistols, 7 rifles, 3 motorcycle combinations** |

So: **yes, an MG34-centric 9-man Gruppe.** Only one man is a pure rifleman; the squad is built around the gun and its drivers.

**Platoon — 39 men:** HQ of 12 (commander, messengers incl. 2 motorcycle messengers, stretcher-bearer, drivers, plus a 3-man 5-cm mortar troop on 2 combinations) + 3 squads of 9. Transport: 1 Kfz.11, 1 Kfz.18, 2 solo motorcycles, 11 combinations. Weapons: 3 LMG, 1× 5-cm mortar.

**Company:** HQ + a Heavy Machine Gun Group (2 MG34, 4 combinations + 2 more for HQ personnel) + 3 rifle platoons + company trains. **Roughly 150 men — this figure is my derivation, not a sourced total.**

*(Aside — the alternative KStN **1111a** of 1 Oct 1938 gave a 12-man squad with **two** LMGs on four combinations, but Kennedy states it was used only in Schützen/Kradschützen battalions **without** an MG Company, i.e. 6. and 8. Panzer-Divisions. **Not 2. Panzer-Division.** Use the 9-man/1-MG version.)*

**Sd.Kfz. 251 are not plausible in this unit and should be removed.** Corroborating evidence:

- Kennedy, citing Niehorster: "in May 1940, in 1st Panzer Division all seven *Schützen* Rifle Companies were halftrack equipped… **The other Panzer Divisions then each had one *Schützen* Rifle Company in halftracks.**" So 2. Panzer-Division's *single* halftrack company was in **Schützen-Regiment 2**, not in the motorcycle battalion.
- General halftrack scarcity: only 1. Panzer-Division was even partly equipped by September 1939; by May 1940 perhaps five divisions were *partially* equipped, and "there were never enough of them to go around."
- Even the 2 × Sd.Kfz. 261 radio cars in the table are doubtful — Kennedy notes these "were not introduced into service until the end of 1940", with the soft-skin Kfz.17 radio car substituted in the early war years.

**Motorcycle models — one correction to your brief:**

- **BMW R12** — correct. 745 cc side-valve boxer, shaft drive; ~36,000 delivered to the Wehrmacht by 1941, the most numerous German military motorcycle of the period. Sidecar could mount an MG34 on a forward mount. **This is your Donchery motorcycle.**
- **Zündapp KS 750 — ANACHRONISTIC.** Accepted by the Wehrmacht April 1940, but **series production only began in spring 1941** (18,695 built 1941–45). It was **not** at Sedan in May 1940. Its BMW rival, the R75, is also 1941+. Remove both.
- Other period-correct options if your asset library has them: Zündapp K800 / KS600, DKW light solos. (Lower confidence on exact 2. Pz.Div. allocations.)

### 1e. Supporting armour — what is plausible

**2. Panzer-Division tank inventory, 10 May 1940** (Panzer-Regiments 3 and 4; figures trace to Jentz, *Panzertruppen* vol. 1, pp. 120–121):

| Type | Qty |
|---|---|
| Panzer I | 45 |
| Panzer II | 115 |
| Panzer III (3.7 cm) | 58 |
| Panzer IV (short 7.5 cm) | 32 |
| Panzerbefehlswagen (command) | 16 |
| **Total** | **266** |

- **Panzer I and Panzer II — highly plausible, and numerically dominant** (160 of 266).
- **Panzer III (3.7 cm) and Panzer IV (short 7.5 cm) — plausible.** The Panzer IV is the historically *correct* bunker-buster here: its short 7.5 cm firing HE at embrasures from behind the railway embankment is exactly what the French accounts describe. Use the **short-barrelled** IV (Ausf. C/D), never the long 7.5 cm.
- **Panzer 35(t) / 38(t) — WRONG. Remove.** 2. Panzer-Division had none. 35(t) = 6. Pz.Div.; 38(t) = 7. and 8. Pz.Div. (This is the aggregator-table error described in the Conflicts section.)
- **Sd.Kfz. 221 / 222 / 223 and Sd.Kfz. 231/232 armoured cars — plausible**, belonging to **Aufklärungs-Abteilung 5** (2. Pz.Div.'s reconnaissance battalion). A 1939 panzer division nominally fielded ~90 armoured cars, all in the recon battalion. Use sparingly and in a screening/recon role, not as assault vehicles.
- **8.8 cm Flak in the direct-fire bunker-busting role is documented at Sedan** — but at the **Gaulier** crossing (1. Pz.Div./Großdeutschland vs Bunker 211), not at Donchery. Use only if you want the flavour and are willing to note the licence.

## 2. FRENCH SIDE

### 2a. Which division and regiments held Donchery

**55e Division d'Infanterie** (Général Lafontaine), **X Corps d'Armée, 2e Armée**, holding the **Sedan sub-sector of the Secteur Fortifié de Montmédy**, on the Meuse from **Pont-à-Bar to the Meuse–Chiers confluence**.

55e DI composition on 10 May 1940:

| Unit | Commander | Strength (10 May) |
|---|---|---|
| 213e RI | Lt-Col Labarthe | 76 officers, 3,081 men |
| 295e RI | Lt-Col Demay | 76 officers, 3,030 men |
| 331e RI | Lt-Col Lafont | 70 officers, 2,718 men |
| **147e RIF** (fortress, attached) | Lt-Col Pinaud | — |
| 11e BM (bataillon de mitrailleurs, attached) | — | — |
| 45e RAD (divisional artillery) | — | — |

**At Donchery specifically: III/147e RIF** under **Crousse**, holding the casemates on the **left (south) bank**. The 147e RIF was mobilised at Sedan in August 1939 with three machine-gun battalions covering the Sedan sub-sector south of the Meuse; **its 3rd battalion was posted at Donchery**. The **1st company of the 11e BM** reinforced the position **south of Donchery**.

Sedan town itself was held by 6 companies of 147e RIF, 3 companies of 295e RI and 6 companies of 331e RI — so **331e RI and 295e RI were in the Sedan sector proper, east of Donchery**, not on the Donchery bank.

**71e DI: NOT at Donchery.** It was inserting between 55e DI and 3e DINA in the **Angecourt / Noyers-Pont-Maugis** sector (east/south-east, toward Mouzon) on 12–13 May; the relief was still incomplete on 13 May. Remove it from your Donchery OOB.

**213e RI** was the 55e DI's reserve regiment, further south; it was the unit committed to the failed Bulson counterattack on 14 May. Not at the riverbank on 13 May.

**Quality note (this is the whole point of the battle):** the 55e DI was **série B** — mainly older reservists, at **~85% of authorised strength**, "inadequate training and incomplete equipment", because the high command judged a German attack through the Ardennes "highly improbable." The 147e RIF specifically "suffered from constant reorganization."

### 2b. French infantry battalion organisation, 1940

Standard **bataillon d'infanterie type Nord-Est**:

- Battalion HQ + command platoon
- **3 compagnies de fusiliers-voltigeurs** (rifle companies)
- **1 compagnie d'accompagnement** (support company): MG platoons, mortar platoon, AT platoon

**Battalion weapons (theoretical):**

| Weapon | Qty |
|---|---|
| FM 24/29 (Châtellerault) LMG | 36 |
| VB rifle-grenade launchers | 48 |
| Mitrailleuse Hotchkiss Mle 1914 (8 mm) | 16 |
| Mortier de 60 mm Mle 1935 | 3 |
| Mortier de 81 mm Mle 1927/31 (Brandt) | 2 |
| Canon de 25 mm antichar | 2 |

**Battalion strength: ~864 men** on the Nord-East establishment (one French source; the same source gives 298 men in the support element). Take ~800–870 as the paper figure.

**Squad (*groupe de combat*): ~12 men**, built entirely around the **FM 24/29**:

- Sergent (group leader), rifle
- Caporal (assistant), rifle
- **FM 24/29 gunner**
- Loader (pistol only)
- 3 ammunition carriers (carbines/rifles)
- Signals/observer
- Remainder: voltigeurs with **Fusil Mle 1886/93 Lebel or Mle 1907/15 (M34)**; **MAS-36** was only partly issued in 1940
- Plus a **VB rifle-grenade** launcher in the group

*(Conflict: another source gives ~11 men per squad and 4 platoons per company. 12 men / 3 sections of 3 groupes is the commoner reading. Use 11–12.)*

**Caveat — 147e RIF was NOT a standard rifle battalion.** It was a *fortress* regiment organised on machine-gun battalion lines: heavier in Hotchkiss MGs and AT guns, much lighter in riflemen, and largely static inside concrete. Build it that way (see §4).

### 2c. Anti-tank and support weapons; the Donchery bunker line

**Anti-tank:**
- **Canon de 25 mm SA-L Mle 1934 (Hotchkiss)** — the standard weapon here. Over 4,400 in French service in 1940; could penetrate every German tank of the campaign except some up-armoured Panzer IVs. **Crew of 7** (chief, gunner, loader, 3 ammunition handlers, driver). The Mle 1937 was the infantry-issue variant. **This is your emplaced AT gun.**
- **Canon de 47 mm SA Mle 1937 (APX)** — much rarer, generally held at divisional AT company level. **I could not confirm any at Donchery. Do not place them.**

**Machine guns:** **Hotchkiss Mle 1914** in 8 mm, both in the bunkers and in the field battalions. The FM 24/29 Châtellerault is the squad automatic. (Both were in service; "Chatellerault" = FM 24/29, "Hotchkiss" = Mle 1914 heavy MG — they are different weapons, not alternatives.)

**The bunker line at Donchery:**
- Type: **"Blockhaus Mle 36 pour mitrailleuse ou canon antichar"** — model 1936 blockhouses for either a machine gun or an anti-tank gun. Plus **MOM-pattern** infantry blockhouses, described as "modest compared to neighbouring subsectors."
- **Billotte** and **Huntziger** type blockhouses were built during the winter of 1939–40.
- Numbers: the 55e DI had **42 bunkers by September 1939, with 61 more added by 10 May 1940** — but **most were incomplete and lacking gun ports.**
- Surviving structures at Donchery show "numerous impacts covering the facade" — the tank direct-fire duel is physically attested.
- **Mines: almost none.** Only 422 of the 2,000 available to the Second Army reached the 55e DI, and some were displaced during bunker construction.

**Artillery:** 55e DI's organic **45e RAD** plus attachments. Across 55e + 71e DI, **174 pieces against 141 German** — so the French were *not* outgunned on paper. Their guns performed well on the morning and afternoon of 13 May (destroying the German boats at Donchery, and 81 of 96 boats at Bazeilles). The collapse came from the **~19:00 Bulson panic**, when a false report of German tanks caused mass abandonment of positions and guns — hours before the first German tank actually crossed the Meuse. Colonel Poncelet, X Corps artillery commander, ordered the artillery back and later took his own life.

> **For a 16:00 mission this matters:** French artillery should be modelled as **present and effective**, not withdrawn. The famous French collapse is a *later-that-evening* event.

### 2d. French tanks at Donchery on 13 May

**None.** The **4e and 7e BCC** (equipped with **FCM 36**) arrived on **14 May**, and were used in the Bulson/Connage counterattack with the 213e RI. Do not place French armour in a 13 May 16:00 mission.

## 3. FORCE RATIO — recommendation

**Historical reality was not battalion vs battalion.** At Donchery the entire **2. Panzer-Division** (~13,000 men, 266 tanks) was committed against roughly **one fortress machine-gun battalion in concrete** (III/147e RIF plus a company of 11e BM). That is a divisional attack on a battalion position — perhaps 10:1 or worse in gross manpower on the sector.

But the *terrain* inverted it. Between 14:00 and 20:00 the Germans could only put across as many men as they had boats for, into flat, open, enfiladed ground, with almost no artillery (24 guns arrived at 17:00 with little ammunition) and a river in the way. The result was a **German failure until the flank was turned by another division.**

**Defensible battalion-vs-battalion framing:**

> **German attacking battalion at ~900 men on paper (959 authorised for a Kradschützen-Bataillon, ~1,021 for a Schützen-Bataillon), but only a fraction across the water at any moment and effectively unsupported by artillery — versus a French fortress battalion of ~500–700 effectives, dug into concrete, at 85% strength, série B reservists, physically stunned by six hours of Stuka bombardment, but with intact interlocking MG/25 mm fields of fire and working artillery support.**

**Gameplay ratio recommendation: roughly 1.5 : 1 to 2 : 1 German numerical advantage**, with the French compensating via fortification, prepared fields of fire, and defensive artillery. Do **not** go 3:1 or higher — it produces an easy walkover that misrepresents the day. The honest headline is: *the Germans had overwhelming force in the theatre and still failed here in daylight.*
