# Squad Formations — German Gruppe & French Groupe de Combat, May 1940

Research for realistic squad formations in the Easy Red 2 "Crossing at Donchery" mission.
Every numeric claim is sourced.

**Read the confidence section first.** Four of the premises in the original brief are wrong:
`Gruppenkeil` does not exist, `Rudel` was abolished before May 1940, the French `RMI 1928/1939`
is not a real document, and the French word `tirailleur` had been removed from doctrine.

---

## CONFIDENCE AND CONFLICTS

### The single most important structural finding

**German 1939-40 doctrine recognised EXACTLY TWO open-order (`geöffnete Ordnung`) formations for
the squad: `Schützenreihe` and `Schützenkette`. There is no third.** From `H.Dv. 130/2b` (1939),
quoted verbatim:

> *"Sie dürfen mit der **geschlossenen Ordnung**, wie, der **Linie**, der **Marschordnung**, der
> **Exerzierordnung** und der (allgemeinen) **Reihe** nicht verwechselt werden, deshalb nennt man
> diese **geöffnete Ordnung**: **a) die Schützenreihe und b) die Schützenkette.**"*

So the taxonomy is: **closed order** = `Linie`, `Marschordnung`, `Exerzierordnung`, `Reihe`
(drill/march formations). **Open order** = `Schützenreihe`, `Schützenkette` (the two combat
formations). Anything presented as a third squad open-order form for 1940 is suspect on its face.

### SOLID — German (two independent source families agree)

The two families are (i) **`H.Dv. 130/2a` / `130/2b`**, quoted in German with page numbers and
figure scans on [forum-der-wehrmacht](https://www.forum-der-wehrmacht.de/index.php?thread/24679-schie%C3%9Fausbildung-heer/),
and (ii) **`The German Squad in Combat`** (US MIS *Special Series No. 9*, 25 Jan 1943), a
translation of a German squad handbook, read in full from
[archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/TheGermanSquadInCombat/TheGermanSquadInCombat_djvu.txt)
(cited `[GSIC p.N]`). Their figure numbering matches exactly (Bild 11/12 = Fig. 11/12), which is
strong mutual corroboration.

**Squad establishment, May 1940 — resolved.** `KStN 131c` dated **10 October 1939**: squad of
**10 men**, **four** squads per rifle platoon, platoon total **48**. Composition: squad leader
(NCO, submachine gun), **6 riflemen**, 1 light machine gunner (pistol + LMG), **2** assistant
gunners (1 pistol, 1 rifle). Per Gary Kennedy, *Organization of the German Infantry Battalion
1938-45* ([bayonetstrength.uk](http://www.bayonetstrength.uk/GermanArmy/GerInfBn/Org%20of%20the%20German%20Inf%20Bn%201938-45.pdf),
p.28 table, p.19, p.55): *"In early October 1939 a set of temporary Rifle Company and Machine Gun
Company KStN were issued, both suffixed 'c'. The key changes were a reduction in the strength of
the Rifle Squad to ten men, accompanied by the addition of a fourth Rifle Squad to each Rifle
Platoon."* Confidence **High**. The platoon also had a **3-man 5-cm mortar troop**
(`[GSIC p.1]`: *"the light-mortar squad (one leader and two men)"*).

**Manual authority — resolved.**
- **`H.Dv. 130/2a`** = *Ausbildungsvorschrift für die Infanterie (A.V.I.), Heft 2: Die
  Schützenkompanie, Teil a — A. Einzelausbildung / B. Die Gruppe* → **squad** formations.
- **`H.Dv. 130/2b`** = *Teil b: Der Schützenzug und die Schützenkompanie* → **platoon**
  formations (`Entfaltung`, `Zug-Keil`, `Zug-Breitkeil`).
- Editions of `130/2a`: **1936, 1937, 1938, then 16 March 1941** (styled *Entwurf*).
  **There was no 1939 or 1940 edition** — the widely-catalogued
  [16.3.1941 issue](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/A76FNRCKXN4QC4PE22RTA6MGUJGEEFKK)
  post-dates the French campaign by ten months and **cannot** be the May 1940 authority.
- **In force in May 1940:** `130/2a` and `130/2b` in their **1937/38** state, as amended by the
  post-Poland ***"Merkblatt für die Gliederung und Kampfweise der Schützenkompanie zu 12 Gruppen"
  vom 13.10.1939*** — the same three-day window as `KStN 131c` (10 Oct 1939). Confidence
  **Medium-High** (the Merkblatt is attested by one forum poster who is inconsistent about its
  exact designation).
- **`H.Dv. 300 Truppenführung`** (1933) is combined-arms leadership doctrine for **larger
  formations**. Squad drill is out of scope — **do not cite it for squad spacings.**

### CONFLICTS FOUND

1. **The `Schützenkette` interval: 5 m or 5 paces (≈4 m)?**
   The German manual says **5 metres**; the MIS translation says **"approximately 5 paces"**.
   With `1 Schritt = 0.80 m`, 5 paces = 4.0 m. **Internal consistency favours ~4 m:** a 10-man
   squad at 4 m gives 9 × 4 = **36 m**, which fits the independently-stated 30-40 yd (27-37 m)
   squad front; at 5 m it gives 45 m, which **exceeds** that front and would breach the stated
   command-and-control limit. **Use 4-5 m as the band; 4.5 m as a single design value.**
   Confidence in the band: **High**. In which end is "correct": **Low** — this is a genuine
   discrepancy between the German text and its US translation.

2. **`Schritt` length.** The `Marschschritt` is **80 cm at 114 paces/min** — Prussian in origin
   and still prescribed ([Gleichschritt](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleichschritt)). But
   `Schritt` as a historical *length unit* in Prussia 1857-71 was 2.4 rheinische Fuß =
   **75.32 cm** ([Schritt (Einheit)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schritt_(Einheit))).
   **Use 0.80 m** ⇒ 5 Schritt = 4.0 m (3.8 m at 0.7532). Confidence **Medium** — 0.80 m is
   documented for the Prussian→Bundeswehr tradition but **inferred** for 1940; no Wehrmacht-era
   regulation stating it was found. *(The French 0.75 m, by contrast, is stated in the 1939
   regulation itself — High confidence.)* Beware `Doppelschritt` (80-160 cm) on large figures.

3. **German platoon frontage varies by source — and partly by attack vs defence.** The
   `[GSIC p.50]` figure *"The front of the platoon is about 200 to 300 yards. Gaps between the
   squads must definitely be covered by fire."* sits in the **defence** section; a worked **attack**
   exercise at `[GSIC p.83]` gives ≈**200 yd**. TM-E 30-451 gives attack **165-220 yd** and defence
   **220-550 yd** ([HyperWar Ch.4](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/Germany/HB/HB-4.html)).
   `miniatures.de` gives `Zug 100-200 m Angriffsbreite`. Best reading: **attack ~150-200 m,
   defence ~200-300 m**. Confidence **Medium**.

4. **French groupe: 11 or 12 men?** `[FV 1939 PAG_16]` adds to **11** (1 sous-officier + 1 caporal
   + 3 fusiliers + 6 voltigeurs). Essentially every secondary source says **12**, and the existing
   `donchery-oob.md` already flags this ("sources say 11 *or* 12; 12 is the commoner figure").
   The regulation is the better authority, but the OCR page needs a clean scan to be certain.
   Confidence: **Medium** for 11 as the regulation figure; **unresolved** overall.

### CONFLICTS FOUND — a widely-cited source is wrong on terminology

**[MilitaryHistoryVisualized](http://militaryhistoryvisualized.com/german-squad-tactics-world-war-2/)
has `Schützenreihe` and `Schützenkette` SWAPPED**, stating verbatim: *"The basic extended order
formations were the Squad Column or 'Schützenkette' and the Skirmish line or 'Schützenreihe'."*
warhistoryonline repeats the inversion and garbles it further. The mechanism is identifiable: MHV's
cited source is the MIS translation, **which contains no German words at all** — so the German
labels are a back-translation that went the wrong way.

The correct mapping, confirmed by the German manual text, by DWDS
([Schützenreihe](https://www.dwds.de/wb/Sch%C3%BCtzenreihe),
[Schützenkette](https://www.dwds.de/wb/Sch%C3%BCtzenkette)), and by the Austrian Bundesheer's use
of `Schützenkette` for the wide formation:

> **`Reihe`** = row/**file** → **depth**.  **`Kette`** = chain → **breadth**.

MHV also renders "5 paces" as **3.5 m**, using the generic Wikipedia "pace" rather than the German
`Schritt`. **If the candidate term list in the brief came from MHV, that is the error source.**

### SHAKY / INFERRED

- **The `l.MG-Trupp` / `Schützentrupp` split was ABOLISHED before May 1940.** This kills a
  tempting number. Kennedy (p.19): *"Under the 1937 KStN the Squad was subdivided into two
  distinct elements, the LMG Troop… and the Rifle Troop… **With the revised organization of late
  1939 this delineation was eliminated.**"* Confirmed from the German side, `[GSIC p.11]`:
  *"The squad is usually employed in combat as a unit. **The division into two groups — a light
  machine-gun group and a rifle group, with different combat missions — no longer applies.**"*
  Confidence **High**, two independent sources.
  **Consequence: the "up to 80 m between the two troops" figure that circulates on
  [miniatures.de](https://www.miniatures.de/tactics-1932-german-infantry.html) is PRE-1939
  doctrine and must NOT be used for May 1940.** That page is titled to **1932**, gives the squad
  as *"der Gruppenführer und 12-14 Mann"*, and cites **no sources at all**. The residual truth is
  only that the LMG still *led* tactically (`[GSIC p.44]`).
- **Which flank leads a wedge.** `Zug-Keil` = **apex forward** (one squad at the point, others
  echeloned back on both flanks). `Zug-Breitkeil` = **broad front, apex to the rear** (two squads
  up, one back). Confidence **Medium** on the shape; the OCR of MIS Figs. 11/12 is too degraded to
  read the exact 4-squad layout, so treat the specific arrangement as **Low**.
- **German man-to-man interval outside the `Schützenkette`.** `[GSIC]` gives **only one**
  man-to-man figure in the whole manual (the skirmish-line one) — grep-confirmed. No separate
  interval is prescribed for the squad column, march order, or squad line. Applying ~4-5 m to the
  `Schützenreihe` is **INFERENCE** (though `miniatures.de` does assert 5 Schritt in file).
- **French man-to-man interval in the attack** is nowhere given as a single number. Derived:
  11 men × 3.75 m = 41 m against the stated *"cinquante mètres environ"* line frontage ⇒
  **use 4-5 m lateral with 3.75 m as the floor.** This is **INFERENCE**, marked as such.
- **Donchery / Sedan casemate spacing is EMPIRICAL, not doctrinal.** **62 works** were built
  Donchery→Noyers-Pont-Maugis on the left bank; 55e DI held Dom-le-Mesnil→Frénois (~15 km of
  river). The German rupture went through a **≈1,800 m gap between casemate 305 (Glaire) and 211
  (Torcy)**. Derived average spacing ≈**210-290 m**. So the honest pattern is
  **200-400 m spacing with occasional 1,000-1,800 m holes** — not a uniform line. Confidence
  **Medium**. No *doctrinal* blockhouse spacing in metres exists; the rule is functional
  (mutual flanking fire), not numeric.
- **All German manual quotations trace to ONE forum poster.** He posts figure scans and page
  numbers, and his Bild 11/12 match MIS Fig. 11/12 exactly — strong cross-confirmation — but he
  is inconsistent in labelling the Oct 1939 document ("D 101" / "Merkblatt 130/2b" /
  "H.Dv. 130/2b von 1939"). A scan of `130/2a`/`2b` or the Merkblatt would be decisive;
  **BArch RHD 4/835** is the identified holding.
- **Not verified:** whether the 1940 *Reibert* carried the formation plates; the numeric French
  *"densité normale"* of occupation; the 11-vs-12-man French figure.
  **Best unexamined modern source:** Passmore, *"Au Contact: French Infantry Tactics in the
  Maginot Line Advance Posts during the Phoney War"*, *War in History* (2025),
  [DOI 10.1177/09683445251329056](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09683445251329056),
  open access at [Cardiff ORCA 176687](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/176687/) — both
  blocked to automated fetch; worth opening by hand.

`Zug-Keil` / `Zug-Breitkeil` (platoon); `Entfaltung` and `Entwicklung` (see below);
`Anschlussmann` (the base man everyone dresses on — in the Gruppe this is **l.MG Schütze 1**,
`[GSIC p.5]`); `Fliegermarschtiefe` / `Fliegermarschbreite` (the two air-threat march
dispersions — TM-E 30-451: *"The Germans are very much concerned about antiaircraft protective
measures and often march in open columns (Fliegermarschtiefe); an advance in deployed formation
(Fliegermarschbreite) is seldom practical."*).

### `Entfaltung` vs `Entwicklung`

Two stages, in this order — the English glosses differ between translators, so use the German:

1. **`Entfaltung`** — the **higher** echelon loosens: companies leave the march route, the platoon
   adopts `Zug-Keil` or `Zug-Breitkeil`. *"Um den Feuerkampf führen zu können muss sich der
   Schützenzug entfalten, damit alle Schützen, einschließlich lMG gleichzeitig das Feuer eröffnen
   können, ohne sich gegenseitig zu gefährden und dem Gegner ein zusammenhängendes Ziel zu
   bieten."*
2. **`Entwicklung`** — the **squad** then deploys into its firing formation. `[GSIC p.5]`:
   *"The deployment of the squad usually follows immediately after the platoon develops."*

### 1d. MG team and leader

**MG team is a tight cluster (`[GSIC pp.17-18]`):** gunner (No. 1) at the firing position and the
squad's base man; assistant (No. 2) *"a few paces to the left or to the left rear and under as good
cover as possible"*, beside the gunner **only** where cover is ample; ammunition-carrier (No. 3)
*"lies to the rear under cover"*. **Internal spacing ~2-4 m** — deliberately tighter than the
rifle line.

*Caveat:* `[GSIC]` contradicts itself on crew numbering. The canonical scheme (pp.5, 37, 38) is
**No.1 gunner / No.2 assistant / No.3 ammunition-carrier**, but p.17 reads *"the assistant (No. 1)
often aided by the ammunition-carrier (No. 2)"* — off by one, almost certainly an error in the
original or the translation. **Use the canonical 1/2/3 scheme.**

**Leader:** no fixed position. Ahead of the squad, reconnoitring, before the firefight; then
behind cover directing *"by calls and signals"*. One worked example gives the second-in-command
following at **50 paces (~40 m)** with the rest of the squad (`[GSIC p.86]`).

## 2. FRENCH GROUPE DE COMBAT, 1940

### 2a. Formations and prescribed intervals — verbatim commands from `[FV 1939]`

| French command / name | English | Prescribed spacing | Metres |
|---|---|---|---|
| `colonne par un` (ordre serré) | single file, close order | *"à un pas environ de distance"* | **0.75 m** |
| `groupe en ligne`, intervalle normal | line | *1 m 15 par homme* | **1.15 m/man** |
| `au coude à coude` | elbow to elbow | 15 cm interval | **0.70 m/man** |
| `colonne par un` (combat) | file, under fire | **5 pas** under effective fire | **3.75 m** |
| `par demi-groupe, DISTANCE (n pas)` | half-groups in depth | `n pas`, leader's choice | n × 0.75 |
| `par demi-groupe, VOLTIGEURS EN TÊTE, DISTANCE (n pas)` | voltigeurs leading | `n pas` | n × 0.75 |
| `par demi-groupe, INTERVALLE (n pas)` | half-groups abreast | `n pas` | n × 0.75 |
| `par demi-groupe, INTERVALLE ET DISTANCE (n pas)` | echeloned | `n pas` | n × 0.75 |
| `FACE À (TEL POINT), EN LIGNE` (`voltigeurs à gauche / à droite / encadrant`) | group on line | *"Le front d'un groupe en ligne est de cinquante mètres environ"* | **≈50 m front** |
| Night, any column | — | *"un pas entre les hommes d'une même colonne"* | **0.75 m** |
| Section: `PAR GROUPES SUCCESSIFS` / `ACCOLÉS` / `EN ÉCHELONS DÉBORDANTS` / `EN TRIANGLE`, plus `FORMATION D'ASSAUT` | — | `INTERVALLE(-DISTANCE) (n PAS)` | n × 0.75 |

**Note the French system is fundamentally different from the German:** the interval is a
**parameter of the command** (`INTERVALLE 10 PAS`), not a fixed doctrinal constant. Only the
*floor* (5 pas under fire, 1 pas at night) and the *envelope* (≈50 m group front) are fixed.

**Envelopes:** groupe front & depth **≤100 m approach / 50 m attack**; section attack **100 m
front, 150 m depth**, defence **≤150 m** (*"en général d'une centaine de mètres"*); company attack
**≤300 m front** (**≤200 m on a fortified front**), ≤400 m depth; battalion defence
**1,000-1,200 m**.

**Leader:** `1 pas` ahead of the base man in close order; in combat *"suffisamment en avant"* when
the advance is covered — **no fixed offset**. The two `voltigeurs-ravitailleurs` *"se tiennent…
peu éloignés du fusil-mitrailleur"* — glue them to the FM team.

### 2b. Defensive dispositions in a fortified sector

- **There is no prescribed man-to-man interval in a French defensive position.** The regulation
  prescribes a **fire plan** instead: **50 m of frontal sweeping fire per FM**, 40 m per 60-mm
  mortar, plus written `consignes de tir` and `consignes de défense`.
  **Reconstruction:** FMs ~**35-50 m apart** inside a ~100 m section front. Marked INFERENCE.
- Positions are explicitly **not** uniformly distributed (quoted above). Do not build an even grid.
- Hierarchy: `poste de combat → point d'appui → centre de résistance → position de résistance`,
  with **`centre de résistance` = battalion** and **`point d'appui` = company down to a section**.
- **Donchery specifics (empirical):** ~**62 works** Donchery→Noyers-Pont-Maugis; average casemate
  spacing ≈**210-290 m**; the decisive weakness was a **≈1,800 m gap** between casemate **305
  (Glaire)** and **211 (Torcy)**. Model **200-400 m spacing with occasional 1,000-1,800 m holes.**

One sourced line each. **The two armies gave opposite advice about artillery** — this is the most
interesting finding in the document.

>

> **They were not.** An exhaustive in-book search of `[GSIC]` found **no** mention of a beaten
> zone, cone of fire, burst radius, or any other numeric derivation of the 5-pace interval. The

### Transition model (trigger cues)

Transitions are **event-driven, not range-driven** — doctrine is explicit that platoon "distances
and intervals are not fixed".

1. `Marschordnung` → **`Entfaltung`**. Cue: entering the attack sector; company leaves the march
   route; platoon takes `Zug-Keil` / `Zug-Breitkeil`. Trigger (verbatim): *"hostile air or ground
   observation, artillery fire, or long-range fire from machine guns is expected."*
   **At this stage each squad is still CLOSE** — the dispersion is *between* squads.
2. → **`Entwicklung`** → `Schützenreihe`. Cue: *"deployment of the squad usually follows
   immediately after the platoon develops."* Only the LMG firing; riflemen held back.
3. → `Schützenkette`. Cue: **the squad's whole firepower is needed** — a firefight is expected or
   joined. Executed "upon signal, order, or command". Reversible: column↔line either way, to
   reduce casualties or to negotiate terrain.
4. → Assault. Cue: fire superiority achieved, or the ordered penetration time. **Formation
   dissolves**; reorganise and un-bunch afterwards.
5. **Artillery interrupt (any state, GERMAN):** prone → then rush **forward in closed formation**
   to the next cover. Never rearward. An *override*, not a formation.
6. **Artillery interrupt (any state, FRENCH):** **widen** interval/distance as far as possible,
   minimum 5 pas. The mirror image of the German rule.

## 5. SANITY CHECK AGAINST THE ±13 m PATHFINDING SCATTER

Your ~13 m σ is **larger than every man-to-man interval in either army's doctrine.** No exceptions.
That is the headline.

### Below the noise floor — statistically invisible

| Thing | Doctrinal value | vs 13 m σ |
|---|---|---|
| DE `Schützenkette` interval | **4-5 m** | 0.31-0.38 σ |
| DE `Schützenkette`, **widest ordered variant** | **10 m** | **0.77 σ** — still invisible |
| DE MG team internal spacing (No.1/2/3) | ~2-4 m | 0.15-0.31 σ |
| DE `Marschordnung` | 1-2 m | ~0.1 σ |
| FR `colonne par un` under fire | **3.75 m** | 0.29 σ |
| FR `groupe en ligne` normal | **1.15 m/man** | 0.09 σ |
| FR night interval | 0.75 m | 0.06 σ |
| FR interval under artillery ("as wide as possible", min 5 pas) | ≥3.75 m, unbounded above | invisible **at the floor**; see below |

**Consequence 1: you cannot render `Schützenreihe` vs `Schützenkette` as a difference in
man-to-man interval.** Both use ~4-5 m. They differ only in **axis** — depth vs breadth.

**Consequence 2: the 13 m scatter is roughly 3× the doctrinal interval, so an isotropic scatter
turns a 32 m file and a 32 m line into the same ~30 m blob.**

### Above the noise floor — measurable, and where to spend the effort

| Thing | Doctrinal value | vs 13 m σ | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DE squad frontage / sector** | 27-37 m defence, 50-100 m attack sector | 2.1-7.7 σ | **Measurable as a squad-level extent.** Place the centroid and axis deterministically, let scatter fill it. |
| **FR `groupe en ligne` frontage** | **≈50 m** (prescribed) | **3.8 σ** | **Measurable.** The single best French number. |
| **FR groupe envelope** | ≤100 m approach / 50 m attack | 3.8-7.7 σ | Measurable. |
| **DE MG alternate firing positions** | **≥50 yd (46 m) apart** | **3.5 σ** | **Measurable, and cheap to implement** — displace the MG between two sited positions. |
| **DE second-in-command trailing offset** | 50 paces (**40 m**) | **3.1 σ** | Measurable — place him deterministically. |
| **DE / FR platoon frontage** | DE ~150-200 m attack; FR 100 m attack / 150 m depth | 7.7-15 σ | **Very robust.** `Zug-Keil` vs `Zug-Breitkeil`, and the French `EN TRIANGLE`, **are** renderable. |
| **FR FM-to-FM spacing in defence** | ~35-50 m (derived from 50 m sweep per FM) | 2.7-3.8 σ | Measurable at the weapon-position level. |
| **FR casemate spacing, Donchery** | 200-400 m, with 1,000-1,800 m gaps | 15-138 σ | Trivially measurable — and the **1,800 m gap is the historically decisive feature**. |
| **DE `Fliegermarschtiefe`** | **2× ordinary march depth** (a multiplier, so unbounded) | ≫ 13 m for any column | **Measurable and trivially cheap** — double the column spacing under air threat. The one air-dispersion rule with a hard number. |
| **DE bound length** | 32 m long-range → 12-16 m close-range | 2.5 σ → 1.0 σ | Long bound measurable; short bound is not. A shrinking-bound model shows only early. |
| **DE MG depth offset in the file** | MG at head of a ~32 m file ≈ 16 m ahead of centroid | 1.2 σ | Marginal but real, **if** you place the MG deterministically. |
| **DE squad-to-squad distance** | 20 paces (16 m) in one worked example; sectors 50-100 m apart | 1.2 σ / 4-8 σ | Marginal at 16 m; robust at sector scale. |

### Recommended implementation strategy

1. **Encode formation identity as AXIS and EXTENT, not as interval.** `Schützenreihe` = a long thin
   distribution *along* the axis of advance (σ_along ≫ σ_across); `Schützenkette` = the transpose.
   **If you can make the scatter anisotropic, that is the single highest-leverage change available
   to you.** With isotropic 13 m noise, the two formations are indistinguishable by construction.
2. **Do NOT reach for the 80 m "MG troop / rifle troop" split** to manufacture a big number. It is
   pre-1939 doctrine, formally **abolished in late 1939** by two independent sources, and the one
   web page carrying the 80 m figure is uncited and titled to 1932. Using it would be an
   anachronism of exactly the kind this document exists to prevent.
3. **Make a few individuals deterministic rather than scattered.** The MG gunner (`Anschlussmann`,
   head of file / centre of line), the second-in-command (tail of column, or 40 m back), and the
   squad leader (forward before the firefight) are all doctrinally *specified* positions, and the
   MG's offset is just large enough to see. Overriding scatter for 2-3 men per squad buys more
   visible historicity than adjusting the interval of all nine.
4. **Do most of the visible work one echelon up.** Platoon frontage (150-200 m DE, 100 m FR) and
   the wedge / broad-wedge / `EN TRIANGLE` arrangements are 8-15 σ and survive the noise
   completely. Squad-level *interval* realism is unrecoverable; squad-level *placement* realism is
   free.
5. **Model the artillery reaction, and model it differently per side.** This is a rare case where
   the two armies' doctrine diverges sharply and the difference is 3-8 σ in your engine:
   Germans **close up and rush forward**; French **spread as wide as possible**. Implementing that
   asymmetry is both historically defensible and clearly visible.
6. **Doctrine is on your side about sloppiness.** *"It is less important that the distances and
   intervals be maintained exactly than it is that the squad avoid losses… attention… directed
   more in the direction of the enemy and less on the formation."* A 13 m scatter around a
   terrain-following squad is arguably **more** historical than a ruler-straight 4 m line — and
   you can cite the manual for that.
7. **For the French defence at Donchery, do not build an even grid.** The regulation says so
   explicitly: *"Ni les postes de combat dans le point d'appui, ni les points d'appui dans le
   centre de résistance… ne sont uniformément répartis sur le terrain."* Irregular 200-400 m
   spacing with a deliberate ~1,800 m hole reproduces the actual failure.
